\begin{abstract}
Influence maximization is dominated by reverse-reachability (RR) sampling, whose guarantees rest
on a coverage representation: for a random set $R$ independent of the seed set $S$, the spread is
$c \cdot \Pr[S \cap R \neq \emptyset]$, a non-decreasing submodular function of $S$. We study
targeted influence maximization under a threshold-dependent overexposure model in which a node
becomes positively active only when the weighted influence it receives from its in-neighbours lands
inside a node-specific window $[\theta^\kappa_v, \theta^\tau_v]$. We first delimit what is and is
not ruled out here. On a one-hop instance with two candidates of weight $0.4$ into a single target,
the model's own window distribution gives $F_D(\{a\}) = 0.48$ and $F_D(\{a,b\}) = 0.32$: the
objective is non-monotone, so no non-negative seed-independent coverage function can match it, and
the standard RR representation is unavailable. The statement is deliberately narrow --- it does not
exclude signed decompositions, restricted-instance algorithms, or Monte-Carlo estimation, and it
does not imply that learning must help. It also requires naming the threshold distribution: under
uniform-threshold linear thresholds the same instance is monotone, so the two readings cannot be
conflated. We then report the regime where this matters, re-measured under the corrected activation
rule at $300$ paired Monte-Carlo trials per cell: saturation is controlled by the seed
\emph{fraction} $|S|/n$ rather than the absolute budget, and across eight graphs spanning mean
degree $4.2$ to $56.0$, out-degree is negatively correlated with the true marginal gain on seven of
them once $|S|/n \ge 20\%$, while a training-free state-conditioned closed form is the better ranker
on all eight. We also correct a claim from an earlier version of this work: the share of candidates
with a negative marginal gain is $0.5\%$ on average and at most $4.0\%$ in any cell, not the
$8$--$35\%$ we reported from twelve trials per cell. Finally we give a measurement protocol for the
model --- five checks, each with the measured magnitude of the error it prevents, because we fell
into all five. The largest is a state-machine error that inflates the measured spread by
$60$--$63\%$ on our densest graph; conflating the model's simplex threshold law with
uniform-threshold linear thresholds reverses the sign of non-monotonicity; and a single candidate
pool moves a degree-to-reference gap between $0.00\%$ and $98.52\%$, so one draw can flip its sign.
We report both what the evidence supports and what it does not.

\end{abstract}

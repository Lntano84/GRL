\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The standard reverse-reachability coverage form does not apply to the named
threshold-window overexposure law. On the exact one-hop instance in \S\ref{sec:coverage}, adding a
second weight-$0.4$ seed lowers the target activation probability from $0.48$ to $0.32$; hence no
non-negative seed-independent coverage function can match this objective. This is a narrow
obstruction: it does not rule out signed decompositions, restricted-instance algorithms, or
Monte-Carlo estimation, and it depends on naming the threshold distribution.

Under the corrected state machine and $300$ paired Monte-Carlo trials per cell, the seed fraction
$|S|/n$ controls the measured regime. Across eight graphs, out-degree is negatively correlated with
true marginal gain on seven graphs once $|S|/n \ge 20\%$, while the training-free state-conditioned
closed form is the better ranker on all eight (\S\ref{sec:regime}). The corrected negative-share
mean is $0.5\%$ and the worst cell is $4.0\%$; low-budget predecessor measurements are not used as
evidence. The accompanying protocol records five checks for state transitions, threshold-law
identification, Monte-Carlo noise, state-size pooling, and candidate-pool repetition
(\S\ref{sec:protocol}).

The submission does not claim a sample-efficient algorithm yet: the pre-fix baseline comparison is
omitted because its state machine and measurement budget were invalid. A difference of monotone
submodular functions does not inherit submodularity, so it cannot by itself license a coverage-based
guarantee. The next experiment must re-derive a sequential quality--cost comparison under the
frozen contract, with priced state acquisition, a Monte-Carlo reference, repeated candidate pools,
and the confounds of \S\ref{sec:limitations} removed. If that gate fails, retain the model/protocol
framing and drop the sample-efficiency title.

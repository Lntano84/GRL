\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Influence maximization (IM) asks for a seed set of size $k$ that maximises expected influence
spread. The dominant algorithmic family is reverse-reachability (RR) sampling
\cite{borgs2014tim,tang2015imm,nguyen2016stop}: sample random reverse walks, observe that
$\mathbb{E}[\sigma(S)] = n \cdot \Pr[S \cap \mathcal{R} \neq \emptyset]$, and solve a
maximum-coverage problem on the sampled sets. RR is attractive because it is both fast in
practice and provably near-optimal. Its guarantees, however, rest on a specific generative
structure: an independent per-edge random mechanism (the live-edge graph of independent
cascade, or the trigger set of linear thresholds) together with a monotone submodular spread
function.

This paper is about a setting where that structure is absent, and about what a practitioner
should do instead. We study \emph{targeted} IM under a threshold-dependent overexposure model
\cite{zhu2026overexposure} motivated by advertising fatigue: a user becomes positively active
only when the weighted influence they receive from their in-neighbours lands inside a personal
window $[\theta^\kappa_v, \theta^\tau_v]$. Too little influence leaves them inactive; too much
makes them \emph{negatively} active, and a negatively active user never promotes the item.
The window is drawn uniformly from the simplex $\{0 \le \theta^\kappa \le \theta^\tau \le 1\}$,
and the exposure a node accumulates is the sum of the weights of the in-neighbours that were
\emph{ever} positively activated.

Our first contribution delimits what this objective rules out, and what it does not.  The
coverage representation behind RR sampling requires the spread to be $c \cdot \Pr[S \cap R \neq
\emptyset]$ for some random $R$ independent of $S$; such a function is non-decreasing and
submodular.  On a one-hop instance with two candidates of weight $0.4$ into a single target the
model's own window distribution gives $F_D(\{a\}) = 0.48$ and $F_D(\{a,b\}) = 0.32$, so the target
is non-monotone and no such coverage function can match it (\S\ref{sec:coverage}).  We state the
scope precisely because it is easy to over-read: this excludes the standard coverage form, and
nothing else --- not signed decompositions, not algorithms for restricted instances, and not
Monte-Carlo estimation, since a sample mean is itself unbiased.  It is also a statement about a
named threshold distribution: under uniform-threshold linear thresholds the same instance is
monotone, so the two readings cannot be conflated.  A weaker version of this argument, phrased as
"RR sets are empty", appeared in an earlier draft of this work and is withdrawn; the probe it
rested on was mis-specified.

Our second contribution is to identify \emph{when} this matters, re-measured under the corrected
activation rule at $300$ paired Monte-Carlo trials per cell (\S\ref{sec:regime}). A naive experiment
measures out-degree against the true marginal gain and concludes that degree is fine --- we made
exactly that mistake, twice. The first time, we swept an absolute budget on a graph where that
budget was at most $26\%$ coverage and mostly far less; measured as a \emph{fraction} $|S|/n$, the
same sweep tells a different story, so any statement about overexposure that does not report
$|S|/n$ should be distrusted. The second time, we read the regime signal out of twelve Monte-Carlo
trials per cell. Across eight graphs spanning mean degree $4.2$ to $56.0$, out-degree is negatively
correlated with the true marginal gain on seven of them once $|S|/n \ge 20\%$ --- NetHEPT, the
sparsest, is the exception --- and a training-free two-hop closed form is the better ranker on all
eight. But the same twelve-trial measurement also told us that $8$--$35\%$ of candidates have a
\emph{negative} marginal gain, and that is false: at $300$ trials the figure is $0.5\%$ on average
with a worst cell of $4.0\%$. The sign flip is real and the negative-marginal share was Monte-Carlo
artefact, and only adequate power separates the two.

An earlier version of this work reported a baseline comparison built on an activation rule that
denied an over-exposed node the ability to turn negative and on Monte-Carlo budgets below the
threshold required for saturated rank measurements.  The corrected rule removes $60$--$63\%$ of
the measured spread on Congress-Twitter, which is larger than every effect reported in that
version.  We therefore omit those tables from the submission and retain only the protocol lesson:
results from this model must be regenerated under the corrected state machine and measurement
contract.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item We show that reverse-reachability sampling is not expressible by a coverage representation
        under threshold-dependent overexposure, by exact arithmetic on a one-hop instance
        (\S\ref{sec:coverage}). The statement is deliberately narrow: it excludes the standard
        non-negative seed-independent coverage form, and nothing else --- not signed
        decompositions, not algorithms for restricted instances, not Monte-Carlo estimation, and
        not the possibility that learning helps. It also fails under uniform-threshold linear
        thresholds, which is why the threshold distribution must be named.
  \item We identify the seed \emph{fraction} as the controlling variable and report a re-measured
        regime characterisation: across eight graphs, out-degree becomes an anti-signal on seven
        once $|S|/n \ge 20\%$ while a training-free state-conditioned closed form is the better
        ranker on all eight (\S\ref{sec:regime}). We also withdraw the negative-marginal share we
        previously reported from twelve trials per cell; at $300$ trials it is $0.5\%$ on average
        and $4.0\%$ at worst.
  \item We give a measurement protocol for this model (\S\ref{sec:protocol}) in the form of five
        checks covering state transitions, threshold-law identification, Monte-Carlo noise,
        state-size pooling, and candidate-pool repetition. These checks define the evidence
        contract for the corrected measurements; withdrawn pre-fix magnitudes are not used.
  \item We show that the upper bound the source model optimises is a \emph{difference} of two
        submodular functions, so even a valid bound would not by itself license a sampling-based
        guarantee (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).
  \item We state eight protocol confounds we found in our own experiment code --- inconsistent
        stopping under a budget of \emph{at most} $k$, unpriced state acquisition, a noisy estimator
        called an oracle, mixed seed sizes in one statistic, a single random candidate pool, splits
        that do not deduplicate, unreported label uncertainty, and weights normalised beyond what
        the model allows --- and for each we say whether it was reproduced or only alleged
        (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).
  \item We report the corrected state machine, the counterexamples that exposed it, and the
        measurement confounds that must be controlled before a sequential quality--cost claim can
        be made (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).
\end{itemize}

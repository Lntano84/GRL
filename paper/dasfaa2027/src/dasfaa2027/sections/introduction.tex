\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Influence maximization (IM) asks for a seed set of size $k$ that maximises expected influence
spread. The dominant algorithmic family is reverse-reachability (RR) sampling
\cite{borgs2014tim,tang2015imm,nguyen2016stop}: sample random reverse walks, observe that
$\mathbb{E}[\sigma(S)] = n \cdot \Pr[S \cap \mathcal{R} \neq \emptyset]$, and solve a
maximum-coverage problem on the sampled sets. RR is attractive because it is both fast in
practice and provably near-optimal. Its guarantees, however, rest on a specific generative
structure: an independent per-edge random mechanism (the live-edge graph of independent
cascade, or the trigger set of linear thresholds) together with a monotone submodular spread
function.

This paper is about a setting where that structure is absent, and about what a practitioner
should do instead. We study \emph{targeted} IM under a threshold-dependent overexposure model
\cite{zhu2026overexposure} motivated by advertising fatigue: a user becomes positively active
only when the weighted influence they receive from their in-neighbours lands inside a personal
window $[\theta^\kappa_v, \theta^\tau_v]$. Too little influence leaves them inactive; too much
makes them \emph{negatively} active, and a negatively active user never promotes the item.
The window is drawn uniformly from the simplex $\{0 \le \theta^\kappa \le \theta^\tau \le 1\}$,
and the exposure a node accumulates is the sum of the weights of the in-neighbours that were
\emph{ever} positively activated.

Our first contribution delimits what this objective rules out, and what it does not.  The
coverage representation behind RR sampling requires the spread to be $c \cdot \Pr[S \cap R \neq
\emptyset]$ for some random $R$ independent of $S$; such a function is non-decreasing and
submodular.  On a one-hop instance with two candidates of weight $0.4$ into a single target the
model's own window distribution gives $F_D(\{a\}) = 0.48$ and $F_D(\{a,b\}) = 0.32$, so the target
is non-monotone and no such coverage function can match it (\S\ref{sec:coverage}).  We state the
scope precisely because it is easy to over-read: this excludes the standard coverage form, and
nothing else --- not signed decompositions, not algorithms for restricted instances, and not
Monte-Carlo estimation, since a sample mean is itself unbiased.  It is also a statement about a
named threshold distribution: under uniform-threshold linear thresholds the same instance is
monotone, so the two readings cannot be conflated.  A weaker version of this argument, phrased as
"RR sets are empty", appeared in an earlier draft of this work and is withdrawn; the probe it
rested on was mis-specified.

Our second contribution is a measurement discipline for the question that follows. A naive
experiment measures out-degree against the true marginal gain and concludes that degree is fine ---
we made exactly that mistake, twice. The first time, we swept an absolute budget on a graph where
that budget was at most $26\%$ coverage and mostly far less; measured as a \emph{fraction}
$|S|/n$, the same sweep tells a different story, so any statement about overexposure that does not
report $|S|/n$ should be distrusted. The second time, we read a regime signal out of rank
correlations computed from twelve Monte-Carlo trials. They were noise: the negative-marginal regime
has a label signal-to-noise ratio below $1.4$ there, and the same measurement at $300$ trials gives
a different answer (\S\ref{sec:experiments}). We therefore state the constraint explicitly --- no
rank correlation in this regime below $\text{MC} \ge 300$, and prefer paired spread comparisons,
which cancel the noise by construction --- and we list the eight protocol confounds
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}) that must be removed before a cost-versus-quality comparison between a
learned and a training-free selector means anything.

An earlier version of this work reported the regime characterisation itself, and the baseline
comparison that followed from it. Those measurements were produced by an activation rule that
denied an over-exposed node the ability to turn negative, and at Monte-Carlo budgets below the
threshold we now require; on Congress-Twitter the corrected rule removes $60$--$63\%$ of the
measured spread, which is larger than every effect that was reported. They are withdrawn, and
\S\ref{sec:experiments} retains them under an explicit marker rather than deleting them, because
the record of what was measured and of why it was wrong is part of what this paper has to offer.
Re-deriving them under a frozen model contract with those confounds removed is the immediate next
step, and we describe what it must control.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item We show that reverse-reachability sampling is not expressible by a coverage representation
        under threshold-dependent overexposure, by exact arithmetic on a one-hop instance
        (\S\ref{sec:coverage}). The statement is deliberately narrow: it excludes the standard
        non-negative seed-independent coverage form, and nothing else --- not signed
        decompositions, not algorithms for restricted instances, not Monte-Carlo estimation, and
        not the possibility that learning helps. It also fails under uniform-threshold linear
        thresholds, which is why the threshold distribution must be named.
  \item We show that the upper bound the source model optimises is a \emph{difference} of two
        submodular functions, so even a valid bound would not by itself license a sampling-based
        guarantee (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).
  \item We report a measurement constraint that we violated ourselves and that we believe
        constrains the area: in the negative-marginal regime the label signal-to-noise ratio is
        below $1.4$ at $60$ Monte-Carlo trials, so rank-correlation claims below $300$ trials are
        not interpretable, and the negative-marginal share is itself the quantity most inflated by
        that noise (\S\ref{sec:experiments}).
  \item We state eight protocol confounds --- inconsistent stopping under a budget of \emph{at most}
        $k$, unpriced state acquisition, a noisy estimator called an oracle, mixed seed sizes in one
        statistic, a single random candidate pool, splits that do not deduplicate, unreported label
        uncertainty, and weights normalised beyond what the model allows --- and record the earlier
        measurements that they invalidate (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).
  \item We withdraw, rather than restate, the empirical regime characterisation and the baseline
        comparison of an earlier version of this work, and we report the corrected state machine and
        the counterexamples that exposed it (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

We list the things that could be wrong, in the order we consider them most likely to matter.

\paragraph{1. A learned scorer does not beat the closed form.}
Having established that $\delta_2$ beats degree and exact greedy, the natural next question is
whether a learned state-conditioned scorer can beat $\delta_2$. On the evidence we collected, it
cannot. We built a dataset of $480$ labelled states on Congress-Twitter ($4$ seed fractions
$\times$ $3$ independent contexts $\times$ $40$ candidates, paired labels at
$\text{MC} = 400$) and evaluated with \emph{leave-one-context-out}, so every score is a
cross-state generalisation score. With $\mathrm{regret} = 1 - (\text{model top-}5\text{ gain}) /
(\text{true top-}5\text{ gain})$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
model & Spearman & regret \\
\midrule
$\delta_2$ (no training) & $\mathbf{+0.6932}$ & $\mathbf{0.5025}$ \\
ridge on the same features & $+0.4255$ & $0.6172$ \\
MLP on the same features & $+0.3652$ & $0.9662$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A feature ablation, replicated on two independently generated datasets, shows that this is not a
matter of insufficient model capacity. Adding threshold-derived features (distance to
$\theta^\tau$, fraction of neighbours inside the band) to the analytic score makes held-out
regret \emph{worse} on both replications ($+0.167$ and $+0.300$), and on the first replication
the combination wins $0/12$ held-out contexts. The single analytic score column is the strongest
single predictor we found.

This should be read as a limitation, not as a theorem: $480$ states is small, and we cannot
exclude that a larger corpus would change the answer. What we can say is that under a
cross-state evaluation protocol, at this scale, the closed form is not beaten, and the
threshold features --- which were our main hypothesis for where extra signal would come from ---
are actively unhelpful. We report it because a paper that shows a strong training-free baseline
should also report that learning did not improve on it.

\paragraph{2. A trap in interpreting feature importance here.}
Pooled across contexts, \emph{every} informative feature correlates with the label at
$|r| \approx 0.85$--$0.91$, including one at $0.907$ that is pure regime identification. This is
because the dataset mixes regimes whose label scales differ by three orders of magnitude
($\approx +200$ when unsaturated, $\approx 0$ when saturated). Any evaluation that does not hold
out a context will therefore report strong, meaningless feature utility. We mention it because
we initially drew exactly that wrong conclusion.

\paragraph{3. Density does not explain the sign flip.}
We would like a crisp graph-level criterion predicting when out-degree fails. We do not have one.
Across the nine graphs the correlation between $\langle k \rangle$ and
$\rho_{\text{degree}}$ is $-0.183$, and Congress-Twitter is a counterexample --- indeed the
correlation moved \emph{towards} zero when the ninth graph was added, which is the opposite of
what a density-based criterion would predict. The structural hint
in \S\ref{sec:regime} (in-/out-degree coupling) is a hypothesis supported by a
$\rho(\text{in},\text{out}) = 1.000$ symmetry in the two collaboration graphs, but nine graphs
is not enough to establish it.

\paragraph{4. Scale and dataset coverage.}
Our largest graph has $15{,}233$ nodes; the source model's datasets are smaller still. We do not
demonstrate behaviour on million-node graphs, and the per-decision cost of obtaining the state
--- one cascade, which is cheap here --- would need re-examination at that scale.

\paragraph{5. The surrogate gap is measured, and it exposes an open question.}
\S\ref{sec:inapplicability} observes that RR \emph{is} applicable to the monotone upper bound
$\lambda(S) \ge \sigma(S)$ used by the source model. We therefore measured the gap
$\lambda(S) - \sigma(S)$ directly, which the source model does not report
(Table~\ref{tab:surrogate}).

Two things follow. First, the bound is \emph{tight where it is useful}: on Congress-Twitter at
$|S|/n = 10\%$ it over-estimates the objective by $9.8\%$, which makes the surrogate worth
optimising --- and $\sigma^\kappa$ and $\sigma^\tau$ are genuine LT influence functions, so RR
sampling applies to each individually. Second, the gap degrades with saturation and, under our
reading of the definitions, \emph{inverts}: $\lambda < \sigma$ in $4$ of $6$ settings. Across a
wider sweep ($4$ graphs $\times$ $3$ fractions $\times$ $30$ trials) the per-realisation
containment that the source model's proof invokes holds in $0/180$ draws and the inequality
$|A| \le |A^\kappa| - |A^\tau|$ holds in $73/180$.

We deliberately do not present this as a refutation. The source states Theorem~6 for the
expectation of $\sigma$ but proves it on one fixed realisation, and at least three innocent
readings remain open: our interpretation of $\sigma^\kappa/\sigma^\tau$ may differ from the
intended one, the two threshold processes may be intended to be coupled rather than independent,
or the proof may be illustrative rather than general. Every counterexample we observe is a node
active in the $\theta^\tau$ process but not the $\theta^\kappa$ process, which is exactly what a
monotone coupling would forbid. Resolving this requires clarification from the authors, and we
flag it as an open question rather than a result. What we can state without qualification is the
measured gap above, and the structural point that follows: $\lambda$ is a \emph{difference} of two
monotone submodular functions, and submodularity is not preserved under differences, so even a
valid upper bound would not by itself license a sampling-based guarantee.

\paragraph{6. Non-monotonicity is regime-dependent, and we once got this wrong.}
We initially reported that the objective is effectively monotone, based on a sweep at
$|S| \le 4000$ on a graph where that is at most $26\%$ coverage and mostly far less. Measured as
a \emph{fraction}, the objective is clearly non-monotone on most of our graphs
(Table~\ref{tab:signflip}). Any statement about overexposure that does not report $|S|/n$ should
be treated with suspicion --- including, historically, ours.

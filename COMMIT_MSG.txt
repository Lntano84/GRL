exp(overexposure): Gate 2c/2d -- zero-noise analytic ranking beats degree 8/8 in the saturated regime

Splits the two jobs that Gate 2 showed must be split.  The analytic score is a good RANKER
but a bad CALIBRATOR of the true marginal (Congress-Twitter, realised state, 60 paired
trials per candidate):

  |S|/n   true mean marginal   analytic mean   ratio    Spearman
  0%          +193.586             +2.852       0.01      0.902
  5%            -0.661             -0.251       0.38      0.667
  10%           +0.167             -0.095      -0.57      0.263
  20%           +0.120             -0.192      -1.60      0.427

It underestimates the marginal by ~100x when unsaturated and has the WRONG SIGN when
saturated, so its zero crossing cannot be a stopping rule -- the first version of the
strategy stopped after one seed and scored 347 against a degree baseline of 364.  Rank
correlation stays positive throughout, so the score remains usable for ordering.

Fix: rank with the closed form, decide stopping from the OBSERVED spread.  With the
threshold windows fixed a cascade is a deterministic function of the seed set (Gate 1d), so
the observed spread carries no estimation noise -- unlike Monte-Carlo greedy, which searches
for the largest marginal using MC=25 estimates precisely where the marginal is ~0.

Congress-Twitter, budget=10, pool=60, 200 paired trials:

  |S|/n   degree top-10   plateau@0 (pure analytic order)
  0%        +362.15          +366.55
  5%          -4.59            -0.50
  10%         -1.97            +2.23
  20%         -5.06            +1.00

Analytic ordering turns the degree damage of -5 into +1, at zero simulation budget.

Robustness (Gate 2d): repeating the whole select-then-evaluate procedure over 8 independent
selection draws, analytic scoring beats degree 8/8 at every seed fraction, with no
counterexample draw:

  |S|/n   degree      analytic mean [min, max]     wins
  0%      +362.72     +366.30 [364.29, 368.05]     8/8
  10%       -2.51      +1.19 [0.48, 2.10]          8/8
  20%       -5.55      +0.23 [0.09, 0.42]          8/8

The degree baseline itself is stable across draws (+5.07..+5.97), which confirms the
degradation at |S|/n = 10% is real rather than a selection artefact.

NOT a universal claim.  On NetHEPT (<k> = 4.23, sparse) degree stays beneficial at every
fraction and actually WINS at |S|/n = 20% (+15.70 vs +11.05):

  |S|/n   degree      plateau@0
  0%      +123.45     +126.21
  10%      +41.80      +49.05
  20%      +15.70      +11.05   <- degree wins
  40%       +7.04       +9.30

Mechanism: after in-weight normalisation delta is driven by in-degree while seed value is
driven by out-degree.  Congress-Twitter is dense (<k> = 55.95), so a high-degree node's
neighbourhood is already pushed into overexposure and adding it drives more neighbours past
theta_tau.  NetHEPT (<k> = 4.23) saturates through seed COUNT rather than node degree, so
degree does not flip sign.  The honest claim is therefore "in dense networks", not
"universally".

Also adds the robustness harness (evaluate_overexposure_selection_robustness.py) that scores
every selection on a shared set of evaluation windows, so only the selection varies.

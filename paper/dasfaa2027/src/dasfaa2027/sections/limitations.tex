\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{No claim of a learned advantage yet.}
The current evidence does not establish that a learned state-conditioned scorer improves on degree
or the training-free $\delta_2$ score.  The earlier predictor and policy-comparison measurements
were generated before the state-machine correction or under an insufficient Monte-Carlo budget,
so they are not reported here.  A valid learned-advantage claim requires held-out contexts,
post-fix labels, repeated candidate pools, and downstream quality--cost evaluation.

\paragraph{The sign pattern is graph dependent.}
The corrected eight-graph sweep finds negative degree correlation in seven saturated graphs, with
NetHEPT as an exception.  We do not attribute this to density: the density--correlation
association is weak and non-monotone.  The plausible in-/out-degree explanation remains a
hypothesis, not a theorem, and larger graph families are needed.

\paragraph{Scale and dataset coverage.}
The largest graph in the current sweep has $15{,}233$ nodes.  We do not demonstrate million-node
behaviour, and the cost of obtaining the exposure state--one cascade in the current prototype--must
be re-evaluated at that scale.

\paragraph{The surrogate is not a certified algorithmic route.}
The source model's $\lambda=\sigma^\kappa-\sigma^\tau$ is a difference of two monotone
submodular functions.  Each component can individually admit a coverage argument, but the
difference need not be monotone or submodular.  The pre-fix numerical surrogate-gap table is
omitted; whether the source theorem is tight under its intended coupling remains open.

\paragraph{Eight protocol confounds.}
The audit found the following issues.  They are listed because the protocol, not a pre-fix number,
is the reproducibility contribution of this section.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Stopping under a budget of at most $k$.} The stopping rule is now explicit and is
        recorded per decision; a policy that cannot evaluate a requested rule refuses it.
  \item \textbf{State acquisition cost.} The exposure state is charged as part of the same cascade
        budget, rather than being silently treated as free.
  \item \textbf{Noisy reference naming.} A Monte-Carlo greedy policy carries its MC budget in its
        name and is not called an exact oracle.
  \item \textbf{Seed-size pooling.} State-dependence ratios are computed inside one seed size and
        reported per size; unsaturated contexts are not pooled with saturated ones.
  \item \textbf{Candidate-pool selection.} Candidate pools are degree-stratified and repeated so
        that a single draw cannot determine the sign of a headroom claim.
  \item \textbf{State deduplication.} Splits are guarded by the canonical seed-set/candidate-set
        state rather than relying on context identifiers alone.
  \item \textbf{Label uncertainty.} Paired standard errors are recorded for marginal labels, and
        rank correlations in the saturated regime are not reported below MC=300.
  \item \textbf{Weight normalisation.} The chosen strategy is recorded explicitly; sensitivity to
        \texttt{sum\_to\_one} versus \texttt{clip\_to\_one} remains required before claiming external validity.
\end{enumerate}

The main limitation is therefore not hidden in an omitted table: the corrected regime result is
valid, but a post-fix sequential sample-efficiency result has not yet been established.  The
paper's title and algorithmic claims must be revisited if the pending quality--cost gate fails.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Influence maximization (IM) asks for a seed set of size $k$ that maximises expected influence
spread. The dominant algorithmic family is reverse-reachability (RR) sampling
\cite{borgs2014tim,tang2015imm,nguyen2016stop}: sample random reverse walks, observe that
$\mathbb{E}[\sigma(S)] = n \cdot \Pr[S \cap \mathcal{R} \neq \emptyset]$, and solve a
maximum-coverage problem on the sampled sets. RR is attractive because it is both fast in
practice and provably near-optimal. Its guarantees, however, rest on a specific generative
structure: an independent per-edge random mechanism (the live-edge graph of independent
cascade, or the trigger set of linear thresholds) together with a monotone submodular spread
function.

This paper is about a setting where that structure is absent, and about what a practitioner
should do instead. We study \emph{targeted} IM under a threshold-dependent overexposure model
\cite{zhu2026overexposure} motivated by advertising fatigue: a user becomes positively active
only when the weighted influence they receive from their in-neighbours lands inside a personal
window $[\theta^\kappa_v, \theta^\tau_v]$. Too little influence leaves them inactive; too much
makes them \emph{negatively} active, and a negatively active user never promotes the item.
The window is drawn uniformly from the simplex $\{0 \le \theta^\kappa \le \theta^\tau \le 1\}$,
and the exposure a node accumulates is the sum of the weights of the in-neighbours that were
\emph{ever} positively activated.

Our first contribution is negative and, we would argue, the most useful part of the paper:
\emph{RR sampling has no domain here}. Two independent obstructions apply. First, because
$\theta^\kappa_v = 0$ is a null event, a node receiving no influence can never be positive, so
the reverse walk that RR sampling is built on has no base case --- every RR set is empty.
Second, and more fundamentally, the exposure score is a set function of the ever-activated
in-neighbours, so once the windows are fixed it is \emph{deterministic} in the seed set; the
only randomness in the whole process is the window draw itself. There is therefore no per-edge
random structure to sample. The classical identity does not merely lose accuracy, it degenerates:
we measure a TIM-style estimate of $0.0$ where the true spread is $111.9$. This is consistent
with the source model's own analysis, which guarantees only a $\gamma/k$ ratio and optimises a
monotone surrogate $\lambda(S) \ge \sigma(S)$ rather than the objective itself.

Our second contribution is to identify \emph{when} this matters. A naive experiment measures
out-degree against the true marginal gain and concludes that degree is fine --- we made exactly
that mistake, twice, before locating the flaw. The controlling variable is the seed
\emph{fraction} $|S|/n$, not the absolute budget. On a large sparse graph, a budget of $200$
seeds is $1.3\%$ coverage and nothing interesting happens; on a small dense graph the same
budget is $42\%$ coverage and the objective becomes genuinely non-monotone. Sweeping the
fraction across nine graphs with mean degree from $4.2$ to $56.0$, we find that out-degree
becomes an \emph{anti-signal}: it is positively correlated with the true marginal gain only on
the sparsest graph, negative on the other eight when $|S|/n \ge 20\%$, and $8\%$ to $35\%$ of
candidates have a negative marginal gain there.

Third, we show that the standard exact reference also fails in that regime. Monte-Carlo greedy
searches for the largest marginal gain using noisy estimates; when the true marginal is near
zero, noise drives its decisions. On Congress-Twitter, adding the highest-degree ten nodes
\emph{reduces} spread by $5.17 \pm 0.40$ while a training-free two-hop closed form adds
$+2.26 \pm 0.24$ and exact greedy adds only $+0.42 \pm 0.31$. The closed form wins on $8/8$
independent selection draws.

Finally, we report a negative result that bounds how much room is left. Having established that
a closed form beats degree and greedy, we asked whether a learned state-conditioned scorer can
beat the closed form. Using leave-one-context-out evaluation over $480$ labelled states, it
cannot: the closed form's regret against the true top-$5$ is $0.34$--$0.50$, and every learned
variant we tried is worse, including one that wins $0/12$ held-out contexts. This is a limitation
of the learned approach at the tested scale, not a proof of impossibility, and we state it as
such.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item We prove and empirically verify that reverse-reachability sampling is structurally
        inapplicable under threshold-dependent overexposure, for two independent reasons
        (\S\ref{sec:inapplicability}). The result does not depend on non-monotonicity.
  \item We identify the seed \emph{fraction} as the controlling variable, and show that
        out-degree degrades to an anti-signal across nine graphs once the network saturates
        (\S\ref{sec:regime}).
  \item We show that exact Monte-Carlo greedy also fails in that regime, because it is
        noise-driven exactly where the marginal is near zero, and that a training-free closed
        form dominates it --- and every other baseline the source model reports against
        (\S\ref{sec:experiments}).
  \item We report a measurement caveat that constrains the whole area: in the
        negative-marginal regime the label signal-to-noise ratio is below $1.4$ at
        $60$ Monte-Carlo trials, so rank-correlation claims below $300$ trials are not
        interpretable (\S\ref{sec:experiments}).
  \item We delimit the remaining gap with a negative result on learned scorers
        (\S\ref{sec:limitations}).
\end{itemize}

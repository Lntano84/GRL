\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Scalable influence maximization.}
The reverse-reachability family underpins the fastest known IM algorithms. Borgs et al.
\cite{borgs2014tim} introduced the reverse-influence-sampling foundation; TIM/TIM+
\cite{tang2014timplus} and IMM \cite{tang2015imm} turn sampled reverse-reachable sets into
maximum-coverage instances with $(1-1/e-\varepsilon)$ guarantees and near-linear expected running
time. OPIM \cite{tang2018online} supports online processing, while Stop-and-Stare
\cite{nguyen2016stop} uses an adaptive stopping rule. GPU-parallel implementations
\cite{shahrouz2021gim,min2020curipples} report speedups of two to three orders of magnitude over
sequential baselines. All of these inherit the identity
$\mathbb{E}[\sigma(S)] = n \cdot \Pr[S \cap \mathcal{R} \neq \emptyset]$, and therefore all of
them require a coverage representation that \S\ref{sec:coverage} shows cannot match this objective
here. Our result is not that these algorithms are slow, but that they cannot be instantiated.

\paragraph{Learning-based influence maximization.}
A complementary line replaces part of the influence-maximization pipeline with learned predictors.
GCOMB \cite{manchanda2020gcomb}, for example, uses a graph convolutional network to prune poor
candidates and a Q-learning stage to construct the solution. Our \S\ref{sec:limitations} records
a boundary in the overexposure setting: the training-free closed form is not beaten by the learned
scorers we tested.

\paragraph{Overexposure and negative influence.}
That excessive exposure can be counterproductive is well documented. The model we study is due to
\cite{zhu2026overexposure}, who introduce the threshold window and derive the
$2\delta(1-\delta)$ activation probability; they establish non-monotonicity and non-submodularity
of the objective and propose an incremental greedy algorithm with a $\gamma/k$ ratio plus an
upper-bound method built on two monotone submodular linear-threshold surrogates. An earlier line
models overexposure through a logistic opinion-formation term inside a targeted-IM objective
\cite{loukides2020laico}, and notes explicitly that because the spread function is
non-submodular, the greedy step cannot be accelerated with lazy evaluation or reverse-influence
sets. Our contribution relative to this line is to give a structural reason --- rather than an
empirical observation --- for why reverse-influence machinery does not apply, and to characterise
the seed-fraction regime in which the resulting failure of degree-based heuristics becomes
visible.

\paragraph{Threshold and complex-contagion models.}
IM has also been studied under threshold dynamics in which adoption requires reinforcement from
multiple neighbours. Reinforcement learning has been applied to such settings
\cite{chen2023rl4ccim}. Our finding adds a boundary condition to this literature: as soon as the
activation rule depends on an accumulated \emph{sum} that must land inside an interval, the
per-edge random structure that sampling-based methods rely on is lost, and the guarantees that
accompany them do not transfer.

\paragraph{CELF, and the cost of a violated precondition.}
\textsc{CELF} \cite{leskovec2007cost} is the standard lazy-evaluation accelerator for greedy
maximisation and is cited as a baseline by the source model. Its saving comes from a
stale-upper-bound argument: if the recomputed gain of the heap head still dominates the
runner-up's stale gain, the head is the true argmax and the remaining candidates can be skipped.
That argument requires the objective to be monotone and submodular. The objective here is neither
--- the source model's Theorem 2 establishes non-monotonicity and its Theorem 3 establishes that
neither submodularity nor supermodularity holds --- so a candidate whose gain has fallen can rise
again later and the skip test is unsound. We therefore implement \textsc{CELF} as the
\emph{exact} greedy it is designed to accelerate; a lazy variant would yield an artificially
weaker baseline whose shortfall came from a violated precondition rather than from the algorithm.
We raise the point because it generalises: every overexposure objective inherits the same
obstruction, and lazy evaluation is one of the few standard accelerations that is \emph{not}
blocked by the absence of reverse reachability, so it is worth knowing that it fails for a
different reason.
